# 2. What Is a Domain?

A domain is a specific subject area or industry in which an AI system operates.

Examples:

| Domain | AI Assistant |
|---|---|
| Healthcare | Medical terminology assistant |
| Education | Exam preparation assistant |
| Finance | Financial education assistant |
| Software | Coding assistant |
| Marketing | AI marketing assistant |
| Engineering | Engineering problem-solving assistant |

A general-purpose LLM can answer questions across many subjects.

A domain-specific assistant is designed around a narrower subject area and a defined set of tasks.

**Example:**

General AI: Answers almost any question.

Marketing AI: Helps businesses understand customers, develop marketing strategies and create promotional content.

# 3. What Is a Domain Task?

A domain task is the specific job an AI assistant must perform within its domain.

For example:

- Domain: Digital Marketing.
- Task: Help small businesses create marketing campaigns.
- Input: Business description and marketing objective.
- Output: Target audience, campaign idea, content suggestions and CTA.

### Domain vs Task

| Domain | Task |
|---|---|
| Digital Marketing | Create Instagram captions |
| Digital Marketing | Identify target audiences |
| Digital Marketing | Develop campaign ideas |
| Digital Marketing | Analyse brand positioning |

**Remember:**

Domain = Where the AI operates.

Task = What the AI does.

# 6. Essential Marketing Concepts

## 6.1 Target Audience

A target audience is the group of people a business wants to reach with its product, service or marketing campaign.

Example:

A clothing brand selling oversized T-shirts might target university students interested in casual fashion.

Useful audience information includes:

- Age range.
- Location.
- Interests.
- Buying behaviour.
- Customer needs.
- Motivations.
- Problems or frustrations.

The assistant should avoid making unsupported assumptions about customers.

## 6.2 Customer Persona

A customer persona is a research-informed representation of a typical customer segment.

Example:

**Persona: University Student**

- Age: 18–25.
- Lifestyle: University student.
- Interest: Casual fashion.
- Need: Comfortable everyday clothing.
- Buying consideration: Style, quality and affordability.

Personas help businesses understand who they are communicating with.

## 6.3 Customer Pain Points

Pain points are problems or frustrations experienced by potential customers.

Example:

A customer might struggle to find comfortable clothing that matches their personal style.

Marketing can communicate how a product addresses a relevant customer need.

## 6.4 Value Proposition

A value proposition explains why a customer should consider a product or service.

It communicates:

- Who the product is for.
- What value it provides.
- What makes it different.
- Why the customer should care.

Example:

"Everyday clothing designed for comfort and effortless styling."

## 6.5 Brand Positioning

Brand positioning describes how a business wants customers to perceive its brand compared with alternatives.

Example:

A clothing brand might position itself around:

- Minimalist fashion.
- Premium quality.
- Affordable everyday clothing.
- Sustainable materials.

Positioning helps create a distinct identity.

## 6.6 Brand Voice

Brand voice is the personality expressed through a company's communication.

Examples:

| Brand Voice | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Professional | Clear and formal |
| Friendly | Approachable and conversational |
| Premium | Refined and sophisticated |
| Playful | Humorous and energetic |
| Minimalist | Simple and direct |

MarketMate AI should adapt its writing to the brand's requested voice.

## 6.7 Call to Action (CTA)

A CTA tells the audience what action to take next.

Examples:

- Explore the collection.
- Learn more.
- Contact us.
- Discover the product.
- Sign up today.

The CTA should match the marketing objective.

## 6.8 Marketing Funnel

The marketing funnel describes stages in a potential customer's journey.

**Awareness:** The customer discovers the brand.

**Consideration:** The customer evaluates the product.

**Conversion:** The customer takes the intended action.

**Retention:** The customer continues purchasing or engaging.

**Advocacy:** A satisfied customer may recommend the brand.

MarketMate AI should understand which stage a marketing activity is intended to support.

# 7. Content Marketing Concepts

## Content Pillars

Content pillars are recurring themes used to organise a brand's content.

Example for a clothing brand:

1. Styling inspiration.
2. Product education.
3. Lifestyle content.
4. Behind-the-scenes content.
5. Customer experiences.

## Marketing Hooks

A hook is the opening line or visual that encourages someone to pay attention to content.

Example:

"Three ways to style an oversized T-shirt."

## Content Formats

Different platforms support different content formats.

Examples:

- Instagram Reels.
- Instagram carousels.
- Instagram Stories.
- LinkedIn posts.
- Facebook posts.
- Email newsletters.

Content should be adapted to the platform and audience.

# 8. Understanding Marketing Campaigns

A marketing campaign is a coordinated set of activities designed to achieve a specific marketing objective.

A campaign generally includes:

1. Marketing objective.
2. Target audience.
3. Core message.
4. Creative concept.
5. Marketing channels.
6. Content plan.
7. Call to action.
8. Performance measurement.

### Example

**Business:** Clothing brand.

**Objective:** Introduce a new collection.

**Audience:** University students.

**Platform:** Instagram.

**Campaign idea:** Everyday outfits featuring the new collection.

**Content:** Short-form videos and styling carousels.

**CTA:** Explore the collection.

# 9. Important AI Concepts Behind MarketMate AI

## 9.1 Large Language Models (LLMs)

An LLM is a machine learning model trained on large amounts of text to understand and generate language.

It generates responses by predicting likely next tokens based on the context.

LLMs can perform tasks such as:

- Answering questions.
- Summarising information.
- Generating content.
- Following instructions.
- Classifying text.

However, fluent output does not guarantee factual accuracy.

## 9.2 Prompt Engineering

Prompt engineering means providing instructions and context to guide an LLM's response.

Example:

"Write an Instagram caption for a clothing brand using a friendly and minimal tone."

The model follows the instruction without changing its underlying weights.

## 9.3 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

RAG combines information retrieval with language generation.

The system retrieves relevant external information and provides it to the LLM as context.

Example:

A business uploads:

- Brand guidelines.
- Product catalogue.
- Pricing information.
- Company description.

MarketMate AI can retrieve relevant information when generating marketing content.

RAG is useful when information changes frequently.

## 9.4 Fine-Tuning

Fine-tuning means providing additional training to a pretrained model using examples.

For MarketMate AI, fine-tuning could help the model learn:

- Consistent marketing response structures.
- Brand voice adaptation.
- Marketing task patterns.
- Audience-focused recommendations.
- Instruction following.

Fine-tuning does not automatically provide current market information or accurate business-specific facts.

## 9.5 Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

SFT is a training method that uses examples of inputs and desired outputs.

Example:

**Instruction:**

Write a marketing caption for an oversized T-shirt brand.

**Input:**

Target audience: University students.

Brand voice: Friendly and minimal.

**Expected output:**

A short, friendly caption that highlights everyday comfort and style without inventing product claims.

The model learns from examples of the desired behaviour.

## 9.6 PEFT and LoRA

PEFT stands for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning.

It refers to methods that adapt a pretrained model without updating all its original parameters.

LoRA stands for Low-Rank Adaptation.

It introduces smaller trainable matrices into selected model layers while typically keeping the original model weights frozen.

This can reduce the resources required for fine-tuning.

# 10. Prompting vs RAG vs Fine-Tuning

These three approaches solve different problems.

| Approach | Main purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Prompting | Guide model behaviour | Write in a friendly brand voice |
| RAG | Provide external information | Retrieve current product details |
| Fine-Tuning | Adapt model behaviour | Learn consistent marketing response patterns |

### Decision framework

- Need better instructions? Use prompting.
- Need external or updated information? Use RAG.
- Need consistent specialised behaviour? Consider fine-tuning.

They can also be combined.

# 11. Defining MarketMate AI's Input and Output

A good AI project needs a clearly defined input and expected output.

### Input

A user might provide:

- Business description.
- Product or service.
- Target audience.
- Marketing objective.
- Social media platform.
- Brand voice.
- Campaign requirements.

### Expected output

Depending on the request:

1. Marketing objective.
2. Target audience.
3. Marketing angle.
4. Content concept.

# 13. Model Evaluation

After fine-tuning, we need to test whether the model actually improves.

We will compare:

- Original base model.
- Fine-tuned model.

Both models should receive the same unseen evaluation prompts and generation settings.

### Evaluation criteria

| Criterion | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Does the response address the request? |
| Creativity | Does it offer useful ideas? |
| Clarity | Is the response understandable? |
| Brand consistency | Does it follow the requested voice? |
| Completeness | Does it include the required information? |
| Instruction following | Does it respect the user's constraints? |
| Factual discipline | Does it avoid invented claims? |

Each criterion can be scored from 1 to 5 using a consistent rubric.

We must use actual results rather than assuming fine-tuning has improved the model.

# 15. Important Lessons

1. A domain-specific assistant needs a clearly defined purpose.
2. A domain and a domain task are not the same thing.
3. Marketing requires understanding customers, not just writing captions.
4. A target audience is different from a customer persona.
5. Brand positioning helps businesses communicate their differences.
6. Brand voice creates consistent communication.
7. Fine-tuning teaches patterns through examples.
8. RAG provides external information.
9. Prompting guides the model through instructions.
10. Model evaluation is necessary to measure actual improvement.

Today's principle:

*Don't start by training a model. Start by defining the problem the model needs to solve.*
